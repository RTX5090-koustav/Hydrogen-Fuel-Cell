Hydrogen fuel cell V6 in a Lexus LS 460

Six fuel-cell cylinders in a 60° V, three per bank, each with its own anode, membrane and cathode, with a battery pack, shown installed in a ghosted Lexus LS 460 sedan. Switch the car off to see the bare prototype on its wheeled cart. Drag to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom.

V6 layout

2 banks × 3 cells, 60°

Each cylinder is one fuel cell. The banks lean outward from the crankshaft busbar, with the air plenum in the valley.

Rails and plenum

H₂ rail + air plenum

An H₂ fuel rail with injectors tops each bank. Compressed air runs through the valley plenum and down a runner to each cathode.

Every cylinder

H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻

Hydrogen splits at the anode, only protons cross the membrane, and they meet oxygen at the cathode to make water.

Busbar and motor

con-rods → crank → motor

Copper con-rods carry electrons from each anode to the crank busbar, through the battery pack and on to the motor, which sits where the gearbox would be.
